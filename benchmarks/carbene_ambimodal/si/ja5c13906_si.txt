Computational methods

Optimization and energy profile calculations. DFT calculations were performed on Gaussian 161.
M06-2X exchange-correlation functional2 and def2-TZVP basis set3, 4 with chlorobenzene (PhCl)
or toluene as solvent was used for geometry optimization and normal mode analysis, Gibbs free
energy calculation and PIO analysis. The solvent model is represented by the self-consistent reaction
field (SCRF) using the SMD Model5. Wavefunction stability tests were performed for all transition
states to rule out the possibility of open-shell singlet pathways. The temperature for thermochemical
correction and entropy contribution to Gibbs free energy were set to 298 K (involving dihalocarbene)
or 384 K (involving dimethoxycarbene). All carbenes studied in this work exhibit stable singlet
ground states, which are more stable than their corresponding triplet states (Table S1).
Quasi-classical simulations. We run quasi-classical simulations at the M06-2X/def2-SVP-
SMD(PhCl/toluene) level of theory from TSs optimized at the same level, using the Progdyn code
developed by Singleton and coworkers.6, 7 For some TSs that have diminishing imaginary
frequencies, e.g. TS-CI2-svp and TS-8C-svp, keyword “maxstep=2” was applied for convergence.
Temperature was set to 298 K (involving dihalocarbene) or 384 K (involving dimethoxycarbene)
for simulations with a time-step of 1.0 fs. Trajectories were terminated when the forming bond
lengths are either lower than 1.6 Å or longer than 3.0 Å, or the simulation time is over 500 fs.
Variational transition state (VTS) search. Variational transition state8, 9 (VTS) search was
performed on KiSThelP for TS1.10 9 points in total (including the structure of TS1) were extracted
from IRC of and near TS1, setting the step size keyword to 5 for the direction to product, and to 20
for the direction to initial vdW complex structure. The calculation was performed at the “variational
transition state kinetic level” on KisThelP.
Bidirectional generative adversarial network for entropic path sampling (BGAN-EPS).
Entropic path sampling (EPS) was performed using a bidirectional generative adversarial network
(BGAN) method developed by Yang et al.11, 12 Training procedures took 50 epochs. Reversal
trajectories (propagating from TS structures to initial vdW structures) were truncated for smooth
sampling around TS structures. For dibromocarbene and diene, 96 trajectories were used for both
(2 + 1) and (4 + 1) in the bond length range of [3.0 Å, 1.7 Å] and [3.5 Å, 1.7 Å] calculated at 298
K. Ensembles of sampling were set to 13 or 18. For dimethoxycarbene and cycloalkenyl aldehyde,
only (2 + 1) was analyzed because (4 + 1) does not have enough trajectory. The bond length range
is [2.7 Å, 1.7 Å], separated into 8 ensembles. Electronic energies, EE, were extracted from
trajectories and averaged in the same range and interval of entropies.
Orbital analysis and visualizations of structures. Intrinsic bond orbital (IBO) analysis was
performed using IBOview13 at the PBE14/def2-SVP level. IBO is a straightforward method of
connecting conceptual electron transfer to visualized orbital overlapping along reaction coordinate
of interest. We targeted IBOs along forming bonds to understand interaction schemes and contrast
different pathways. Principal interacting orbital (PIO) analysis was performed using the code
developed in Lin’s group.15, 16 Wavefunction information were calculated with the NBO7 setting.
We observed symmetries of PIO on each fragment, which can help to judge whether forming a bond
between certain atoms is symmetrically allowed or prohibited. Contributions of PIOs reflect on
strength of interactions between fragments defined, therefore may denote the tendency of bonding.
Electron populations help to identify electrophile and nucleophile. Visualization of MO and PIO
was performed on Avogadro.17 3D pictures of structures were drawn using CYLview.18

   As shown in Table S1, the singlet states of dihalocarbenes are stabilized by conjugation between
the halogen lone pairs and the vacant p-orbital of the carbene center. This conjugative stabilization
diminishes from F to I, leading to a corresponding decrease in the ΔGTriplet-Singlet value from

stronger lone-pair conjugation, resulting in the greatest singlet stabilization among all carbenes
examined in this study.

   We also tested the possibility of a triplet pathway because a triplet intermediate IM-Triplet can
be located. However, because the triplet state of dibromocarbene is 13.1 kcal/mol higher in free
energy compared with its singlet state, a barrier of 18.9 kcal/mol is required for entering the triplet
pathway via TS1-triplet. Then the ring-closure via TS2-Triplet from IM-Triplet requires a barrier
of 25.3 kcal/mol. However, in experiment19 the possibility of having diradical intermediates is
capped as the reaction gives a decent yield of 79%. Therefore, we ruled out the possibility for the
triplet pathway.
   As quasi-classical simulations on TS1 lead to a recrossing ratio of 19%, we searched the
variational transition state VTS1 around TS1 and ran 100 trajectories on VTS1 (Figure S3B). The
recrossing ratio is indeed decreased to 8% but simulations start to yield recrossing trajectories
between the 2 products (5% recross back to the initial state while 3% are between the 2 products).
Meanwhile, the (2 + 1)/(4 + 1) ratio of trajectories is 0.69 : 0.31 for VTS1, in close consistency with
the ratio of 0.66 : 0.34 for TS1, indicating similar dynamics behaviors for both VTS1 and TS1.
Therefore, we still run quasi-classical simulations on TS1 for better reproducibility and more
complete analysis for trajectories.

Figure S4: Fitting the data point of the (2 + 1)/(4 + 1) cycloaddition into a linear correlation between product ratio
and bond length difference.


The data point of ambimodal cycloaddition between dibromocarbene and diene was plotted against
an linear correlation (Figure S4) between the ratio of competing products and differences of lengths
of corresponding bonds in ambimodal TSs of pericyclic reactions,20 and a reported outlier.21
According to the linear correlation, ambimodal phenomenon would diminish if the difference of
competing bond lengths exceeds 0.4 Å. However, in the case of ambimodal TS1, the difference
between the bond forming (4 + 1) versus the bond forming (2 + 1) reaches 0.57 Å—well beyond
the proposed threshold—yet the product ratio of (4 + 1) from QCMD remains substantial at 0.34,
rendering the newly found point as another salient outlier. This deviation from the expected trend
suggests that other factors may contribute to the observed ambimodality.

  With a hypothesis that low LUMO energy and small C1-C4 distance favor the (4 + 1) pathway
(Figure S5A), we studied the cycloadditions involving other dienes. In cycloaddition between 5C
and dibromocarbene (Figure S5B), the situation is similar to that of 7C and dichlorocarbene (Figure
                                                                       8
5). As 5C has lower LUMO energy than that of 7C, trajectories show that the ambimodal TS, TS-
5C-h1 leads to significantly higher ratio of (4 + 1) product. However, because the C1-C4 distance
of 5C is also longer than that of 7C, hampering the C4-C5 orbital overlapping, TS-5C-h1 is
kinetically unfavored compared with another competing pathway via TS-5C-h2, which yields only
(2 + 1) product for 0.5 kcal/mol. Therefore, the experimentally observed (2 + 1) to (4 + 1) ratio is
increased to 0.96 : 0.04. For 8C (Figure S5C), although its C1-C4 distance and LUMO energy are
consistent with those of 7C, however, its bond length difference between C4-C5 and C2-C5 in the
ambimodal TS is increased to 0.60 Å, decreasing its (4 + 1) product ratio. The (2 + 1)/(4 + 1) ratio
of trajectories is 0.10 : 0.90, in decent agreement with the experimental ratio. Noticeably, 6R (Figure
S5D) has both smaller LUMO energy and C1-C4 distance than those of 7C, and indeed trajectories
starting from TS-6r-h1 show (4 + 1) is now the main pathway, but in previous experiments only (2
+ 1) product was observed.22 This is because the ambimodal TS-6r-h1 is kinetically unfavored as
its Gibbs free energy is 2.8 kcal/mol higher than that of TS-6r-h2. The relatively higher energy of
TS-6r-h1 may arise from steric repulsion between bromine and hydrogen.

   Similar to the case of cycloaddition between dibromocarbene and diene, the 1st and 2nd PIO pairs
correspond to the simultaneous interactions between HOMO (carbene) and LUMO (olefin), LUMO
(carbene) and HOMO (π-orbital of olefin). However, according to IBO analysis of example
trajectories (Figure 7), the ambimodality of TS-2OMe is achieved by the competing interactions
between LUMO (carbene) with either HOMO (π-orbital of olefin) or HOMO – 1 (lone pair of
carbonyl oxygen). Therefore the 2nd PIO pair facilitates the formation of C2-C5 bond, i.e. (2 + 1)
product. The 3rd PIO pair incorporates the HOMO – 1 (olefin), but its contribution to the overall
interaction is as trivial as 1.3% involving presumably in hydrogen bonding with a methyl group of
carbene. Its electron population on the 2 fragments also has a steep difference of 0.01 versus 1.99,
also indicating a weak interaction. Such a trivial component of orbital interaction in TS-2OMe
favoring (4 + 1) reflects on the low (4 + 1) ratio obtained through trajectories, and emphasizes the
importance of entropic intermediate in directing ambimodal pathways.

   From previous studies of Houk et al.20, We took the pericyclic reaction between hexatriene and
butadiene as an example for comparison. In Figure S11A, at the level of theory used in this work,
the bond length difference is 0.46 Å (0.37 Å in literature), shorter than the value of 0.57 Å in the
case of dibromocarbene and diene, while the product ratio of (6 + 4) (with longer forming bond)
versus (4 + 2) (with shorter forming bond) is 0.053 : 0.947 (0.048 : 0.952 in literature). PIO analysis
(Figure S11B) of ambimodal TS-64-42 reveals that, according to orbital symmetries, both the 1st
and 2nd PIO pairs allow interactions for both (4 + 2) and (6 + 4) pathways as of depicted in Figure
S11C. However, although the 2 pairs have comparable contributions, 48.1% and 47.2%, (6 + 4) is
overwhelmed by (4 + 2) meaning neither of them prefer (6 + 4). Therefore, although PIO can help
to deduce the orbital interactions originating ambimodal TSs, predicting product ratios remains
difficult. Apart from contributions and electron populations of vital PIO pairs, other key information
including orbital distributions on atoms that form competing bonds, and/or indices for orbital
interactions, are required for better predict product ratios using orbital analysis methods.
